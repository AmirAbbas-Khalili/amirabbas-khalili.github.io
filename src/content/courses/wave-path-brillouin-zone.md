A lattice structure repeats a primitive unit cell in real space, where its physical geometry is defined. Each degree of freedom (DOF) describes an independent motion, and an eigenfrequency is a natural vibration frequency with a corresponding mode shape showing how the structure moves. Reciprocal space describes the wave vectors kx, ky, and kz, and the first Brillouin zone contains the distinct wave vectors needed to study the periodic structure. Following a wave path through high-symmetry points reveals how its vibration frequencies change with wave direction, helping us understand wave propagation and resonance, which can occur when excitation approaches a natural frequency.
